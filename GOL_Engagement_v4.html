Elementary K–4 · Strong Roots & i-Ready 3 campuses
What your team is navigating

Teachers are flagging students and i-Ready data is running every cycle — but there is no shared decision protocol that moves what the teacher knows and what the data shows into the hands of the fellow delivering the support, before the week starts. Fellows arrive and do their best with what they have. The students who need the most targeted help get the most generalized response — not because anyone failed, but because the connection between the tools has never been built.

What your team walks away with
Strong Roots Routing Protocol — decision rules for grouping, movement, and escalation built directly from i-Ready diagnostic data
Fellow Session Guide — what the diagnostic says, what the session targets, what the teacher already tried — ready before Monday
Escalation Pathway — a named trigger and a named next step for the students who need more than the current block provides
Principal Monitoring Sheet — three leading indicators to read each Monday before the week starts
Student Transition Record — auto-generated from data your ES team already entered; travels with every student to middle school in August

Discovery through launch
A doable path to fall
Discovery begins in June. Each campus builds in sequence. Your system is running its first full diagnostic cycle with the protocol active by the first week of school. Here is what each stage looks like, who is in the room, and what EJC is doing at every point.
1
Discovery June · Weeks 1–2 ~2 hrs per campus

One focused conversation per campus. No assumptions are made about what is working or what needs to change. The goal is to understand each campus's actual schedule, actual staffing, and how i-Ready data currently moves — or doesn't — from assessment to action.

Who is in the room
Chiffon (CSO) — network context, priorities, what success looks like across all seven campuses
Campus Principal — schedule reality, staffing, current Strong Roots structure
Dean of Fellows — how fellows are currently assigned and how Whetstone observations run
What comes out of this stage
Campus-specific build brief for each school
Confirmed i-Ready data structure and export pathway
Staffing map: who owns what in the current system
EJC project lead
Leads each discovery session. Listens before building. Leaves with enough to build a protocol mapped to your campus's reality, not a template. Chiffon reviews the build brief before any protocol work begins.
2
Build June–July · Weeks 3–7 Sequential by campus

Each campus gets its own protocol built to its own staffing and schedule reality. Elementary campuses build first. Middle school follows in sequence. High school closes the build. Chiffon reviews each protocol before it goes to the campus principal — nothing is delivered to a building without her sign-off.

Who is involved
Chiffon (CSO) — reviews and approves each campus protocol before delivery
Campus Principal — reviews draft, confirms scheduling and staffing fit
EJC — builds each protocol, integrates Whetstone alignment at MS level, generates Student Transition Records from ES data
What comes out of this stage
Strong Roots Routing Protocol — per ES campus
Diagnostic-to-Assignment Protocol — per MS campus
30-Day Triage Response — HS campus
Student Transition Records — ES to MS, auto-generated
Principal Monitoring Sheet — all campuses, shared language
EJC project lead
Builds each campus protocol off the discovery brief. Sends a draft to Chiffon for review before the campus principal sees it. Incorporates revisions and confirms fit. The Student Transition Record is generated at the close of the ES build — ready before orientation.
3
Orientation Late July · Week 8 90 min per campus

One working session per campus — teachers, fellows, and the principal in the same room. This is not a training day. It is the session where the protocol becomes theirs. Every person who will touch the system in the fall leaves the room knowing exactly what they do, when they do it, and what happens next.

Who is in the room
Campus Principal / AP — owns the monitoring sheet; understands escalation triggers
Classroom Teachers — understand their role in the routing protocol and the two-sentence flag
Fellows & Dean — walk through the assignment protocol and the movement trigger
HS: Counselors — introduced to the early warning list and the 30-day triage response
What comes out of this stage
Role-specific one-page guide — every person leaves with exactly what they need, nothing more
First-week activation checklist — confirmed assignments, confirmed groupings, confirmed monitoring sheet
Chiffon's network monitoring view — active before the first day of school
EJC project lead
Leads each campus orientation session. Walks every role through their specific guide. Confirms that every person can answer: what do I do Monday morning? Does not leave until that question has a clear answer for each role in the room.
4
Launch & First Cycle August–October · Weeks 9–14 Active support

The system runs its first full diagnostic cycle with the protocol active. This is not a handoff — EJC stays present through the first cycle. A check-in at day 30 addresses anything that needs adjusting before the second cycle begins. A check-in at day 60 confirms the system is running without ongoing support.

Who is involved
Chiffon (CSO) — reviewing network monitoring view; flagging anything that looks off across campuses
Campus Principals — running the monitoring sheet; escalating through the named pathway
Fellows & Teachers — executing the routing protocol; flagging questions through the principal
What this stage confirms
Identification window is operating — students flagged in days, not weeks
Fellow assignments are running from diagnostic data, not availability
Chiffon can see across all seven campuses from one view
Day 60: system runs without ongoing EJC support
EJC project lead
Available through the first cycle for questions and adjustments. Leads the day-30 check-in — one focused conversation with Chiffon to review what the monitoring view is showing and make any protocol adjustments before the second cycle. Leads the day-60 confirmation that the system is self-sustaining.
